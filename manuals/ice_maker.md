# Frostline IM-200 Countertop Ice Maker

## Product Overview
Portable countertop ice maker with a 2.2 L water reservoir, gravity-fed pump, ice basket,
and a sealed refrigeration system (compressor, condenser, refrigerant circuit, R600a —
flammable refrigerant). Makes 9 bullet cubes per ~10-minute cycle. Mains powered.

## Safe Troubleshooting — steps you MAY guide the customer through
- **No power**: check the socket with another appliance; make sure the unit sat upright for
  at least 2 hours after delivery or transport before first switch-on (compressor oil must settle).
- **"Add water" light with water present**: drain via the bottom plug, rinse the reservoir,
  refill with room-temperature water to the MAX line, and clean the small float sensor at the
  back of the reservoir with a soft cloth.
- **Small, hollow, or slow ice**: use colder water, keep the unit at least 15 cm from walls so
  the side vents can breathe, and keep it out of direct sunlight; room temperature above ~32 °C
  degrades ice quality — this is normal.
- **"Ice full" light with empty basket**: wipe the two infrared sensor windows on the inside
  walls of the ice chute with a dry cloth.
- **Scale build-up / cloudy ice**: run a cleaning cycle with 1:1 white vinegar and water, then
  two cycles of fresh water; discard the first batch of ice afterwards.
- **Water leaking onto the counter**: check the bottom drain plug is fully seated and the unit
  is on a level surface; don't fill above the MAX line.
- **Ice tastes stale**: discard ice older than 24 hours (the basket is not a freezer — ice
  melts and recycles), and run a cleaning cycle.

## DO NOT — Specialist Only (NEVER instruct the customer to do these)
- **Refrigerant circuit**: never puncture, recharge, solder, or repair any part of the sealed
  system — R600a is flammable and escaping refrigerant is hazardous.
- **"Not cooling" / compressor not running**: no customer-side fix exists — never suggest
  opening the unit; book an engineer.
- **Sealed compressor housing**: never open, tilt-test with the cover off, or bypass the
  compressor relay/overload protector.
- **Opening the outer shell or base plate**: exposes the condenser coil, mains wiring, and
  refrigerant lines.
- **Internal wiring, thermal fuse, control board, or pump replacement**.
- **Clearing ice jams with knives or any metal tool near the evaporator prongs**: the
  evaporator contains refrigerant and punctures easily.
